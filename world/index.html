Remember who has the key to the lighthouse.

Four modes sit beside the manuscript: Codex, Outline, Timeline and Notes. Switch with the toolbar or ⌘1 to ⌘5, with Write on ⌘1.

Codex

Your story bible.

Keep an entry for every character, place, item, faction, concept and glossary term. Each kind starts with fields that suit it, such as a character’s age, goal and flaw or a place’s region and climate, and you can add your own. Entries also hold aliases, tags, notes and, for characters, a role from protagonist to minor. Edits save as you go, and drag to reorder.

Appears in
Each entry lists the scenes that mention it. Click one to open the scene.
In this scene
The inspector’s World tab shows the codex entries in the scene you’re writing, and updates as the scene saves.
Find characters and places with AI
Let AI read the manuscript and suggest entries. Tick the ones you want and accept.
Update from the manuscript
Ask AI to propose a new summary for an entry based on what the book now says about it.

Chat and continuity checks use the codex too, so the AI knows your world as well as your prose. See the AI tools.

Outline

See the whole book as a board: one column per chapter, a card per scene with its summary, status and word count. Give scenes a status from idea through outlined, drafted and revised to final, and filter the board by status. Double-click a card to open the scene, or ask AI to summarize it from the card’s menu.

Timeline

Track when things happen in the story, in story time rather than chapter order. Each event has a “when” in your own terms, like Spring, Year 3, and can link to the scenes and codex entries involved. Add an event after another, reorder events, and double-click to edit.

Notes

Everything that isn’t manuscript: research, ideas, reminders. Pin the notes you need most. Notes can belong to a scene, and a scene’s notes appear in the inspector while you write it.
